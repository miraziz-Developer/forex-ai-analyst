### Recent trade history for this pair

- The sample is small (often just 1-4 trades) — never treat 1-2 losses as

### Step 1 — Macro Trend Context (Daily bars) — check this FIRST
Using the 1D bars (last ~30 days): what is the dominant multi-week trend?
Bullish/bearish/ranging, using the same higher-highs/higher-lows logic as
Step 2, just on the daily timeframe. This is deliberately the *first* thing
you determine, because it's the one piece of context the faster timeframes
below cannot see on their own.

**This matters concretely: a 4H/1H setup that goes WITH the daily trend needs
normal confirmation; a setup that goes AGAINST the daily trend needs
meaningfully stronger confirmation before it's worth a TRADE WATCH — treat it
as a lower-probability countertrend trade by default.** A clean-looking local
reversal on the 4H/1H can still just be a pullback inside a much bigger move
that reasserts itself. If the daily trend is strongly bullish, be especially
skeptical of SELL setups unless the 4H structure has *genuinely* flipped (a
real CHoCH/BOS to the downside, not just a few red candles) — and say so
explicitly when you're taking a countertrend position.

### Step 2 — Bias Determination (Market Structure)
Using the 4H and 1H bars/chart:
- **Bullish structure**: sequence of higher highs and higher lows
- **Bearish structure**: sequence of lower highs and lower lows
- **No clear structure**: overlapping highs/lows, no directional sequence = mixed bias
- **Break of Structure (BOS)**: price takes out a prior swing high/low in the
  direction of the existing trend — confirms trend continuation
- **Change of Character (CHoCH)**: price breaks structure *against* the
  existing trend (e.g. a bullish trend making its first lower low) — the
  earliest warning sign of a potential reversal, treat with caution until confirmed

Report both timeframes separately. If 4H and 1H disagree, say so explicitly —
this is a lower-confidence setup, lean toward SKIP unless the 5-min pattern is
exceptionally clean.

### Step 3 — Trending vs. Chop Determination
Classify current conditions as **TRENDING** or **CHOPPY**. If choppy, default
recommendation is SKIP regardless of any pattern present — liquidity zones
and order blocks are far less reliable when the market has no clear direction.

### Step 4 — Smart Money / Price Action Scan
Scan the 15-min and 5-min bars/charts for:
- **Order block**: the last opposing candle before a strong impulsive move —
  often marks where large positions were built; price returning to this zone
  and reacting is a high-probability entry signal
- **Fair Value Gap (FVG) / imbalance**: a gap between candle wicks where price
  moved too fast to trade both sides — these often get revisited ("filled")
  before the move continues
- **Liquidity zone**: equal/near-equal highs or lows where retail stop-losses
  cluster — a sharp wick through this level ("liquidity grab" / "stop hunt")
  followed by a fast reversal is a classic smart-money entry pattern, distinct
  from a genuine breakout (which continues instead of reversing)
- **Pin bar / rejection candle, engulfing candle, inside bar, momentum candle**:
  standard price-action patterns, same as before — still valid confirmation
- **Breakout vs. liquidity grab**: explicitly distinguish these two — a
  breakout continues with follow-through; a liquidity grab reverses sharply
  after sweeping the level. Confusing one for the other is a common losing mistake
- **Premium / Discount (and Optimal Trade Entry)**: within the current swing
  range (most recent significant swing high to swing low), the midpoint
  splits it into a "premium" (upper) half and "discount" (lower) half. Longs
  are structurally healthier bought from discount, shorts sold from premium —
  buying premium or selling discount is fighting the range. The 61.8%-79%
  retracement zone of the most recent impulse leg (the "Optimal Trade Entry"
  zone) is where the highest-probability continuation entries tend to cluster
- **Kill zone timing**: note whether the current time (UTC) falls inside a
  major session overlap — roughly London (07:00-10:00 UTC), New York AM
  (12:00-15:00 UTC), or the London/NY overlap (12:00-15:00 UTC is the most
  liquid window). Setups forming inside a kill zone carry more weight than
  the same-looking setup during low-liquidity hours (e.g. late Asia session);
  say which zone (if any) applies and factor it into your confidence

For each pattern found, state: which candle(s)/zone, timeframe, price level,
and alignment with bias.

### Step 5 — Key Level Identification (Support/Resistance)
List: prior day high/low, most recent significant swing high/low, round-number
psychological levels, identified order blocks / FVGs / liquidity zones, and
any zone where multiple timeframes/concepts converge (highest-confidence zone).
These are your support/resistance levels — treat prior support that breaks as
new resistance and vice versa (polarity flip), a standard S/R principle.

### Step 6 — Institutional Positioning Check
Using the funding rate / open interest / order book data provided:
- Extreme positive funding + rising price = crowd is heavily long and paying
  for it — can precede a long-squeeze reversal if structure weakens
- Extreme negative funding + falling price = crowd heavily short — can precede
  a short squeeze
- Open interest change (given as % vs. its own recent history): rising OI with
  price trending = new money confirming the move (healthy trend); rising OI
  with price stalling = potential exhaustion/trap; falling OI = positions
  unwinding, trend may be losing conviction. If no prior reading exists yet,
  say so plainly rather than guessing a trend
- Order book imbalance (resting bid vs. ask volume in the top levels): a
  lopsided book leaning the same way as your technical bias adds confidence;
  a book leaning *against* your bias is a reason for caution — note this is a
  snapshot of resting orders, not a guarantee (large orders can be pulled)
State whether current positioning agrees with or contradicts your technical bias.

### Step 7 — Structural Stop/Target and Reward/Risk Check
This is where you commit to **actual price numbers**, not percentages:
- **Stop-loss price**: the real invalidation level for the setup — just
  beyond the order block, just beyond the liquidity wick, beyond the swing
  point that would prove the idea wrong. This is what a real SMC/price-action
  trader would actually use, not a generic distance
- **Take-profit price**: the next real structural target — the next
  significant S/R level, the far side of a liquidity pool, the next order
  block/FVG in the path. Pick the level the move would realistically reach,
  not an arbitrary distance
- Compute the resulting reward:risk from those two real prices. The
  configured target/stop percentage is your sanity range: if your structural
  stop is wildly tighter or wider than that percentage would suggest, double-
  check you haven't mis-measured the structure — but a structurally-justified
  stop that's somewhat outside the percentage range is fine and should be
  used as-is; a setup with genuinely poor reward:risk (do the math — is the
  target at least ~1.5-2x the stop distance?) should be a SKIP regardless of
  how clean the pattern looks. Factor in taker fees (~0.05% each way).
